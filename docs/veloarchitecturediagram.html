VELO — the architecture is a boundary

One side proves. The other side stays sealed. What crosses is marked explicitly by the contract author, and the compiler enforces it.

The private side

All analysis happens on the examiner's machine. Raw evidence never touches the network — not even encrypted, because encryption today is not privacy forever and a court can order destroyed what a permanent network cannot delete.

The boundary

disclose() is not a team convention: it is a compile error if missing. The four private witnesses enter the circuit and none of them leaves — all that is published is a hash over the six elements.

The public side

The commitment is the anchor. A local custody chain with entries deleted no longer matches the published value, and an attacker cannot rewrite the ledger. The tip is the anchor point; the ledger is the anchor.

What the diagram does not promise

The circuit proves a relationship between the witness values it receives, not that an engine ever ran on real evidence. That gap is called G1, we named it ourselves in round 2, and it closes with an accredited-examiner credential.
